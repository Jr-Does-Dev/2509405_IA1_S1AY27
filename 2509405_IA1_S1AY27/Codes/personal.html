Reflection: The Richest Man in Babylon

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

This chapter lays out seven steps for building wealth: save a tenth of everything you earn,
control your spending, make your money work for you, protect it from loss, own your home,
plan for future income, and keep increasing your ability to earn. Reading it, I realised I had
already done some of this without knowing it. When I was working in the call centre, I saved up
for a laptop because I had a goal in mind, and that discipline made me feel closer to it.
The cure that stood out to me most was the last one, increasing your ability to earn.
I was making money at the time, but it felt like a dead-end job, and choosing to apply to college
was my way of investing in my skills instead of staying comfortable. The chapter also made me
see that controlling my expenses is something I still need to work on, especially separating
what I need from what I simply want.

The Five Laws of Gold

The five laws explain how gold behaves. It comes to those who save at least a tenth of what they earn,
it works for owners who invest it wisely, it stays with those who take advice from people
experienced in handling it, it slips away when put into things the owner does not understand,
and it flees from those who chase unrealistic returns or trust schemers. The third law
reminded me of how I made my college decision. I read articles and spoke to graduates before
choosing, and the advice lined up, which gave me the confidence to go through with it.
Moving forward, I want to apply the same approach to my money by learning before I invest,
asking knowledgeable people for guidance, and staying away from anything that promises
quick riches.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway is that wealth is built through small, consistent habits and good decisions,
not luck. Looking back, I could have made some of my choices sooner, and the book showed me
that waiting costs just as much as a bad decision. I plan to start saving a portion of
everything I earn now, even if the amount is small, and let it grow over time.
